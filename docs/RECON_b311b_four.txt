RECON -- BATCH 311 BRIEF B, the four targets NOT reconstructed
==============================================================

Anim_Gaia was taken to a measured park (scratch_elev/b311b/PARK_Anim_Gaia.c,
SIZE EXACT, count 992/990, aligncmp 579 of 990 = 58.5%).  The four below were
NOT reconstructed.  What follows is MEASURED structural reconnaissance only --
no candidate was compiled for any of them, and no figure here is an objcmp or
aligncmp figure.  Every number below came from a grep of the reference .s and
can be re-derived in one command.

This is deliberate: the brief says two well-understood functions beat five
shallow ones, and after Gaia there was not budget to take a 1026-1474
instruction function to the same depth.  A half-written candidate with an
honest-looking objcmp line would have been worse than this.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
0.  THE TRIAGE IN THE BRIEF IS WRONG ABOUT WHICH OF THESE IS HARDEST, AND
    THE AXIS THAT CORRECTS IT IS THE FRAME, NOT THE INSTRUCTION COUNT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

The brief ordered these by instruction count and said to work smallest-first.
On the frame greps that ordering is wrong by a wide margin:

  function             insns  frame   aggregates              spilled scalars
  Anim_ScreenShatter   1051   0x38    NONE                    8
  Anim_Gaia  (done)     936   0x48    2  (0x30, 0x38)         10
  Anim_Neptune         1474   0x74    4  (0x28,0x48,0x58,0x64) 18
  Anim_Kirin           1026   0xb0    6 + three `mov rX, sp`  22
  Anim_Boreas          1340   0x14c   2  (0x3c, 0x4c)         12

Anim_ScreenShatter is 115 instructions LONGER than Anim_Gaia and has a
SMALLER frame, NO aggregates at all and TWO fewer spilled scalars.  It is the
most tractable of the five and should have been second in the brief, not
third.  Anim_Kirin, nominally the second smallest, has 22 spilled scalars and
SEVEN stack aggregates -- it is the hardest of the five by this measure and
should be last.

Anim_Boreas's 0x14c frame is mostly one large declared array, not spill
pressure: only 12 scalars are spilled and the two `add rX, sp` aggregates sit
at 0x3c and 0x4c, so roughly 0x100 bytes above 0x4c is a single local buffer.
Find its element stride before writing anything.

RECOMMENDED ORDER FOR THE NEXT BATCH:
  1. Anim_ScreenShatter   (smallest frame, no aggregates, best oracle)
  2. Anim_Boreas          (12 scalars; the big frame is one array)
  3. Anim_Neptune         (4 aggregates, 18 scalars, 169 high-reg mentions)
  4. Anim_Kirin           (6 aggregates + 3 `mov rX,sp`, 22 scalars)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  A FREE PER-FUNCTION SIGNAL WORTH TAKING BEFORE WRITING ANY LOOP:
    THE LOOP-COMPARISON CENSUS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

This came out of Anim_Gaia and it paid there, so it is recorded as a method.
Count the signed-comparison branches against `bne` in the reference:

  function             bne   ble/blt/bge/bgt
  Anim_Gaia            (all loops `bne`)      -- every one of 13 loops
  Anim_Boreas           48    32
  Anim_Kirin            33    25
  Anim_Neptune          35    55
  Anim_ScreenShatter    16    48

In Anim_Gaia EVERY loop closes on `bne`, and writing `i < N` instead of
`i != N` cost a `cmp #N-1 / ble` or, worse, a COUNTDOWN (`sub / cmp #0 / bge`)
which also changes which quantity loop.c picks as the giv.  Converting all
thirteen was part of a 505 -> 528 of 990 move.

Anim_ScreenShatter is the OPPOSITE case -- 48 signed branches against 16
`bne`.  Do NOT import Gaia's `!=` rule into it.  The census is per-function
and it is one grep; take it first and write the loops to match.

Caveat on the raw counts: `bhi`/`bls` on a biased subtraction
(`sub r3, #K / cmp r3, #N / bhi`) is an unsigned range test
`(unsigned)(x - K) <= N`, not a loop bound, and both appear here.  Count only
the branches that close a backward edge.

3.  WHAT ANIM_GAIA PROVED THAT TRANSFERS, WITH ITS FIGURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Full detail is in the header of scratch_elev/b311b/PARK_Anim_Gaia.c.  The four
that are most likely to matter here:

(a) `base + 0x7828` IS NOT A NAMED LOCAL in this bank.  Naming it buys an
    extra spill slot and the whole frame and every slot reference goes wrong
    -- 471 -> 505 of 990 to remove it.  Anim_Ragnarok's `slot` local is the
    documented EXCEPTION, not the pattern.  All four targets read 0x7828
    (Kirin 6 pooled references, ScreenShatter 1, and both others), so this
    applies to every one of them.

(b) A STACK AGGREGATE INITIALISED FROM A GLOBAL IS A STRUCT ASSIGNMENT, not
    subscript stores -- 557 -> 579 of 990 on Gaia, the largest single move
    after the frame.  The tell is loads-both-then-stores-both
    (`ldr r4,[r3,#4] / ldr r3,[r3] / str / str`), which is `emit_block_move`,
    plus DIRECT sp-relative stores with `add rX, sp` materialised LATER.
    Anim_Kirin has the identical shape at `Data_edad8` -> sp+0x5c/0x60 and
    Anim_ScreenShatter at `Data_edae0`; both are 8-byte `.incdata`
    objects -- verified: rom_eda78.s has `Data_edad8, 0xedad8, 0xedae0` and
    `Data_edae0, 0xedae0, 0xedae8`.
    This is the single most transferable finding of the batch and it is free
    to apply.

(c) THE SPILL-SLOT ACCESS-COUNT TABLE IS THE RANKING INSTRUMENT, not the
    aligned figure.  On Gaia a declaration reorder moved the aligned figure by
    TWO encodings -- indistinguishable from noise, and the first pass wrote it
    off as inert -- while actually correcting a 7-encoding slot misassignment.
    The slot map caught it; nothing else did.  Build the reference's
    `offset -> access count` table FIRST (one grep), carry it as a column
    beside size, count and aligned, and drive on it.
    NARROWING OF A RECORDED FINDING: docs/band-800plus.md section 3 says
    declaration order is inert.  That was measured on constants placed by
    LOCAL-alloc and does NOT cover reload spill slots, where declaration
    order DOES set the assignment order.  Both statements are true.

(d) A MASK CONSTANT MUST STAY INT-WIDE OR IT WILL NOT BE HOISTED.
    `q[9] = (q[9] & ~0xc) | 4;` lets fold distribute the narrowing convert
    over the AND; the mask becomes 0xf3, which is a one-instruction constant,
    so loop.c does not hoist it.  Splitting the statements so the AND is not
    under the convert keeps -13, which costs `mov`+`neg`, which is what makes
    loop.c move it to the preheader.  THE MECHANISM IS PASS ORDER -- loop runs
    before combine, so once the constant is a hoisted register the AND is
    reg-reg and combine can no longer narrow it.  Any byte-field read-modify-
    write with a negated mask is exposed to this.

4.  ANIM_SCREENSHATTER -- THE SETUP REGION IS ALREADY READ, SO START HERE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Lines 1-330 of the function were read.  Facts, so the next pass does not
re-derive them:

- IT ALLOCATES ITS OWN BUFFERS and does not read `iwram_3001eec`.  Three
  `galloc_iwram` calls open the function: tag 0x27 size 0x782c -> slot
  sp+0x28, tag 0x28 size 0x80<<7 -> sp+0x24, tag 0x29 size 0x302 -> (return
  value unused in a slot).  Those are exactly the three sizes
  docs/battle-animations.md tabulates, which confirms the reading.  Five
  `gfree` calls at teardown.
- `iwram_3001e74` is read into sp+0x14 and `iwram_3001e74 + 0x8c` into r9,
  which is then held in a HIGH register across the whole setup and written at
  `+0xc` and `+0x10`.  r9 is the candidate for a long-lived source local.
- THE SLOT MAP, reference access counts, scalars descending:
      0x28  the tag-0x27 state block   (the hot pointer)
      0x24  the tag-0x28 render buffer
      0x20  --
      0x1c  blitB = gPtrs[0xbc/4]
      0x18  blitA = gPtrs[0xb8/4]
      0x14  iwram_3001e74[0]
      0x10  --
      0x0c  --
  Only EIGHT scalars and no aggregates, so the declaration list is short.
- BOTH blitters are built, unlike Kirin: `BuildDraw2DFuncEx(0x2e, 7, 7, 3, 1)`
  then `(0x2f, 7, 7, 3, 2)`.  NOTE the differing fifth argument, 1 then 2 --
  that is not the `BuildDraw2DFuncs` helper's 2-then-3 pattern, so it must be
  written out, not delegated.
- THERE ARE TWO IDENTICAL `gDMATaskCount` / `REG_IME` PUSH SEQUENCES, the
  HBlank register queue docs/battle-animations.md describes for `Func_ea0d8`
  (entries of {value, register, control}, capacity 0x20, pushed under an
  REG_IME guard).  Each is a guarded `stmia r3!, {rN}` triple with a
  `cmp r2, #0x1f / bgt` capacity check.  THEY ARE DUPLICATED IN THE ROM, so by
  the bank's "duplicated ROM code means duplicated source" rule (stated in
  src/rom_c9000/rom_ceb30_c_c_c_b.c) they are duplicated SOURCE, not a helper
  -- write both out in full and let gcc cross-jump the common suffix.
  The two pushes are {0x7741, 0x5000000, 0x20000} and
  {0x1f81, REG_BG1CNT, 0x20000}.
- A 0x10 x 8 NESTED FILL writes halfwords to 0x6003800 (a BG tilemap):
  the inner body is `(base + row*0x200 + k*0x200) | (col*2 + k*2)` sign-
  extended through `lsl #16 / asr #16`, so the carrier is a SIGNED SHORT and
  the `lsl/asr` pair is NOT redundant -- it is `(short)` in the source.
  Both loops close on `bne` against 8 and 0x10 respectively, so these two are
  `!=` even though the function is `<`-dominant elsewhere.  The census is per
  LOOP, not per function.
- `Func_80008d4` and `Func_80008d8` are the two fills
  docs/battle-animations.md lists (`Func_8d4(dst, n)` and
  `Func_8d8(dst, n, v)`), both reached through a register, so both are
  `CopyFn`-style function-pointer locals in the source -- the
  `CopyFn copy = Func_...;` idiom from src/rom_c9000/rom_e0524.c.
  THE DELIVERED GAIA PARK PROVES THE PLACEMENT RULE for these: assign the
  pointer AFTER any intervening call, never before, or the veneer register
  comes out call-saved (`_call_via_r5`) instead of the ROM's scratch one
  (`_call_via_r3`).  ScreenShatter wants r3, r4, r5 and r7 veneers; four
  distinct registers means four distinct live ranges, so expect one pointer
  local per region, not one for the function.
- The 0x21-iteration loop at `.Lcbe5a` reads `.Lee016` and `.Lee037` as
  unsigned bytes in parallel, shifts both left 16, stores them at +0 and +4 of
  a 0x1c-stride record and stores `(x + 0xffe00000) >> 2` and
  `(y + 0xffc40000) >> 2` at +0xc and +0x10.  Both tables ARE 33 bytes
  (verified above), so the pairing is confirmed.
