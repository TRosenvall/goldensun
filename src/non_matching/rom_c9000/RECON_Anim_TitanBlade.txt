FROM BATCH 308B, THE TWO LEVERS THAT MOVED THE MOST AND WHY THEY APPLY HERE
===========================================================================
Both of this batch's big steps came from reading REGISTERS, not meaning, and
both are cheap to try on a first candidate:

1. THE SPILL-SLOT MAP IS THE DECLARATION LIST.  Reload assigns spill slots in
   increasing pseudo number, expand_decl creates pseudos in declaration order
   after the parms, and the frame grows DOWN -- so sort the reference's spilled
   scalar offsets descending and that IS the declaration order, parameters
   first.  Address-taken aggregates sit ABOVE all of them, also declaration
   order descending.  On Anim_Djinni this pinned every offset in the function
   before any register work; on Anim_CriticalHit a slot that sat BELOW a
   gcse-created pseudo proved a local I had declared could not be declared at
   all.

2. A REGISTER THAT REPEATS ACROSS DISJOINT LOOPS DEFINES THE VARIABLE
   PARTITION.  Worth 471 encodings on Anim_Djinni and 20 on Anim_CriticalHit.
   Tabulate which hard register each loop's walker and each loop's counter uses;
   loops that share a register share a variable, loops that do not each need
   their own.  A COUNTER IN r9/r10 is the tell that it is shared across MORE
   loops than you have written, because gcc-2.96 ranks allocnos by roughly
   log2(n_refs)/live_length -- summing disjoint ranges LOWERS priority into the
   high registers -- and Thumb-1 then pays two extra instructions per loop for
   the placement.  Our stream being SHORT is the signature.

Ragnarok is 584 encodings across many loops, so item 2 is likely to be worth
more here than anything else on the list.
